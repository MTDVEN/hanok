PHASE 1

The valley and the houses

one roof per $100k

This is the feature Zico briefed: a Clash-of-Clans field that gains a hanok every $100k. The engine exists; this phase is art, ingestion, placement and tuning.

PHASE 2

The villagers

two sheets · ~250 lines

The one measurement that drives every decision here

At the shipped plate width (1080px) a hanok is 130–190px across, which puts the scale at about 21px per metre. A person is therefore roughly 10 × 25px on desktop and 4 × 9px on a 390px phone.

That is small enough that detail is not a risk — it is a liability. Faces, fingers and fabric folds collapse into a dark smudge, and a dark smudge against a washy plate is the exact mismatch that got the first sprite set rejected. Everything below optimises for silhouette and tone, and nothing else.

2.1

Two sheets, eight figures, one walk cycle

VEN

PROMPTS.md step 08. Sheet A is the left leg forward, sheet B the right; alternating them is the walk. Eight separate rolls would never agree with each other — one sheet guarantees the cast shares an angle, a light and a density.

Generate B only once A is accepted, using A itself as the image reference, changing exactly one sentence. Figure 3 on sheet B must be recognisably the same farmer as figure 3 on sheet A; if the cast comes back reshuffled, re-roll B rather than renumbering A.

2.2

Cut and prep

VEN

Eight loose crops per sheet, matching numbering, into art/village/people/ as p-01a…p-08a and p-01b…p-08b. Both sets through prep.html, which trims each figure to its own bottom edge — the same ground-line contract the buildings have.

A and B must trim to the same ground line. prep.html trims at the lowest surviving pixel, so one stray speck under either frame makes that walker hop 3px on every step, forever.

2.3

Route mode in tools/place.html

Nothing in the code can infer where the painted lanes are, so the routes get traced by hand once — click a polyline along a lane, press enter, click the next. It prints a WALKS array in the same normalised master coordinates PLOTS uses, so cropY() remaps it and ?plate= keeps working.

Four to six routes is plenty: the trunk from the bottom edge, each main lane, and one or two spurs. Routes that share an endpoint let a walker turn a corner instead of only shuttling back and forth.

2.4

The layer

Walkers are DOM images, not canvas — because they must be occluded by the buildings, and the buildings are already DOM images sorted by depth.

.village__plate
  svg#villageField     inline fallback, hidden once art loads
  img.v-base           the plate
  img.v-house   × N    z-index from y (new — one line in buildArt)
  div.v-walker  × N    z-index from y; two frames inside
    img.f-a / img.f-b
  svg.v-birds          above everything, pointer-events:none

Buildings currently rely on DOM append order for depth, which cannot interleave with anything added later. Giving both buildings and walkers z-index = round(y × 1000) makes depth a number instead of a position, with no visual change to the existing render.

2.5

Motion, per frame

  • Position: walk a normalised distance s along the route's polyline, arc-length parameterised after the crop remap so speed is even on screen.
  • Perspective: k = 0.60 + 1.52·(y − 0.59), clamped to 0.55–1.05 — fitted to the same three ROW_K anchors the buildings use, so a walker beside a house is scaled by the same ladder.
  • Transform only, never left/top. Width is set once; k lives in the transform. That keeps the loop compositor-side, the same discipline journey.js's draw() holds at ~0.011ms/frame.
  • Facing: scaleX(-1) from the sign of travel.
  • Speed: ~0.6× real walking pace, about 16px/s at k=1, so crossing the whole valley takes the better part of a minute. ?walkspeed=.
  • Step: frames swap every ~420ms, scaled inversely with speed. Swap by toggling a class on the wrapper — never by changing src.
  • Bob: 1.2px at k=1, one arc per step, folded into the same transform so it scales with distance.
  • Rest: at a route end, pause 1.5–5s, then turn around or take a route sharing that endpoint. One walker in four is a stander — parks at a clearing for 8–20s with no leg swap.
2.6

Population follows the roofs

walkers = min(MAX, 1 + round(roofs × 0.6)) — twelve on desktop at a full field, six at ≤600px. So the lanes fill as the village fills, and at $0 there is a single traveller on the trunk path arriving at an empty valley.

This is the first thing on the page that makes the villager tally stat visible rather than decorative. New walkers fade in when a roof rises, the way roofs do.

Which figure walks which route is seeded from the contract address, like the buildings — the same village, with the same people in it, every visit. Their positions are not: motion is time-based, so nobody stands frozen where you left them.

2.7

When it runs

One shared requestAnimationFrame loop for walkers and birds, started by an IntersectionObserver on .village__field and stopped when it leaves — literally “once the valley is on the user's screen”. Also stopped on visibilitychange, and dt is clamped to 50ms so a tab returning from the background doesn't teleport everyone across the field.

Reduced motion: the loop never starts. Walkers are placed at sensible points along their routes and stand still, birds hold one pose. A still village with people in it is a better static state than an empty one.

2.8

Failure behaviour

Same house rule as the rest of the section: nothing is mounted until the file has loaded, because an <img> with a dead src gets Chrome's broken-image glyph painted on top of the art underneath — the bug that bit the journey twice.

  • No figure files → no walkers, no glyphs, birds still fly.
  • Only sheet A survives → one-frame walkers with the bob. Degraded, still fine.
  • No WALKS authored → no walkers. Never fall back to straight lines across open grass.
  • The life layer mounts after the buildings and can never delay or block them.

The known limit, stated up front

If the village is ever run in states mode (state-NN.png full paintings) a walker cannot be occluded by a building, because the buildings are baked into the plate. Phase 1 step 1.7 removes states, so this only matters if that decision is reversed — in which case walkers are gated to sprite mode rather than left to walk through walls.

PHASE 3

The birds

no art · ~90 lines

Why these are drawn and not generated

A bird here is 8–14px wide. At that size it is two ink strokes — a flick, not a picture — and a painted PNG cannot flap its wings. Drawing them buys a continuous flap and a glide instead of two or three fixed frames, in the same ink as everything else.

This is the one deliberate exception to “VEN makes the art, the code makes the machinery”, and it's the same allowance the fallback layers and structural marks already have. If they look wrong on screen the fallback is a three-pose bird sheet in the custom style, and the swap costs nothing.

3.1

The mark

One shallow M — two quadratic arcs meeting at the body, stroked in --ink, no fill, round caps. Drawn once at unit size; every bird is that one shape under a transform.

vector-effect="non-scaling-stroke" so the ink line keeps a constant weight no matter how small the bird is. A distant bird should be smaller, not drawn with a finer nib — that's what makes it read as the same hand as the plate.

3.2

The flap

Vertical scale on the wing group: f = 0.35 + 0.75·|sin(ωt)| at about 5Hz. One attribute write per bird per frame, no path rewriting.

Birds don't flap continuously — they beat and then glide. Bursts of 3–6 beats, then 1.2–2.5s holding f ≈ 0.9 with a slow bank. That rhythm is most of what sells it; a metronome flap reads as a logo animation.

3.3

Where they fly

Across the upper band of the plate only — 5% to 42% of its height, which is the misty ridge and crag, where birds belong and where nothing else is happening. Not across the whole viewport: a bird drifting over the manifesto reads as a widget, not as weather. ?birdzone=screen exists to compare, and stays in the code either way.

Slow lateral drift, 30–70s to cross, with two out-of-phase sine terms on y so no two paths look alike. Size and opacity both track height — higher is smaller and fainter (0.22 → 0.5 opacity). One spawn in three brings a companion 25–40px off, because birds travel in loose company.

Off the edge, they respawn on the other side after 4–15s. Without that delay the sky is a treadmill.

3.4

Free consequence of the plate's edge mask

The plate already feathers its four edges by 4% so it reads as a drawing on the sheet rather than a tile. Birds are children of that plate, so they dissolve at the frame edge instead of popping — no extra code, and worth verifying rather than assuming.

3.5

Counts and switches

Four on desktop, two at ≤600px. Under reduced motion, three static birds at fixed points — a still landscape painting with birds in it is completely normal, and better than an empty sky.

PHASE 3B

Sleeping houses

BUILT 2026-08-16 · HANDOFF §9o

Built — every step below is in, as specced

VEN brought this forward on 2026-08-16 (“small and subtle just like in clash of clans”). Who sleeps and each house's rhythm: js/village.js (seeded, ~30% of dwellings, AWAKE excludes gate/pavilion/storehouse). How a Z moves: one keyframe block in css/site.css (.v-zzz) — peak opacity .55, three staggered glyphs, 8.5–12.5s cycles, phase per house. ?sleep=0 off, ?sleep=N share; IntersectionObserver gates it to the visible valley; no Z's under reduced motion; hidden below 640px per 3B.3.

Why this is the cheapest life on the page

VEN: “some Z's floating above certain houses to indicate that there are villagers sleeping inside.” It is the same trick as the birds and it inherits all of their machinery — drawn in code, ink on the plate, in the observer-gated rAF loop that already exists. But it is better than the birds per line written, because it says something the picture cannot: a house with a Z above it is occupied. The field stops being architecture and becomes a village with people asleep in it, and the “villager tally” stat above it acquires a meaning you can see.

A Z is a glyph, not a drawing — three strokes, and the site already has a display face (Song Myung) doing exactly this kind of mark. So this is not the “VEN makes the art” exception the birds needed; it is typography.

3B.1

Which houses sleep

Seeded from the contract address, exactly like the building assignment — the same houses must be asleep on every visit, or a holder who comes back finds a different village, which is the whole thing the seeding exists to prevent. Roughly one house in four, chosen from the plots currently standing, never the gate and never the pavilion (an open pavilion has no inside to sleep in).

Re-rolled with ?seed= like everything else. A new roof appearing must not change who is asleep.

3B.2

The animation

Three Z's per house at decreasing size, rising from just above the roof ridge, each fading out as it drifts up and slightly to one side. Staggered so they never leave together, and the cycle restarts on a long interval — 6–10 seconds of nothing between bursts, varied per house. Constant Z's read as a broken loop; occasional ones read as sleep.

Anchored to the sprite's own box, so a Z sits over its roof at any plate width without a second coordinate table. Transform and opacity only, no layout per frame — same discipline as the walkers.

3B.3

Scale is the risk, and it is measurable

At the shipped plate width a house is 55–135px across, so a legible Z is about 7–9px — and about 3px on a 390px phone, where it will read as a speck or as dirt. Check it at 390px before tuning anything else. If it does not survive, the answer is to show Z's only above --w-wide rather than to make them bigger, because a Z large enough to read on a phone is a Z that dwarfs its own roof.

3B.4

Gating and switches

Same IntersectionObserver and document.hidden gate as the birds and walkers — nothing animates until the valley is on screen. ?sleep=0 turns it off, ?sleep=N sets the share of houses. Under reduced motion, no Z's at all rather than static ones: a frozen Z above a roof is a typo, where a still bird is just a bird.

PHASE 4

Ship

before the token launches
4.1

Measure the loop

Budget: ≤0.15ms/frame with 12 walkers and 4 birds, measured the same way the journey was (~0.011ms/frame with every polish layer on). Twelve transforms and four attribute writes should land far under it; measure anyway, because the whole architecture here is “build once, write attributes per frame”, and a regression to per-frame layout would be invisible in review and obvious on a phone.

4.2

Optimise the art

The plate, six buildings and sixteen figures to .webp. One trap carries over from the states work: optimize.js gives every file its own median-cut palette. Do that to p-03a and p-03b independently and the figure changes colour on every step — the same defect that destroyed the state crossfade, at 25px. A/B pairs share a palette or skip quantisation.

4.3

Build rules

tools/build.js ships from an explicit allow-list, so add art/village/people/* and the life-layer code, and keep the two 1024² master sheets, PLAN.html, prompt-sheet.html, tools/ and the root masters out. Also: build.js wipes dist/ including dist/.vercel/, which unlinks the Vercel project on every build — preserve it across the wipe.

4.4

Then the rest of the page

The village stops being the weakest thing on the page the moment phase 1 lands. The next target is the manifesto and the ledger — untouched since session 3, and after the journey's backdrop fades out the whole lower half of the page is four centred stacks on flat paper with no change of rhythm across ~2,900px.
